The Book

From Moro resistance to the Siege of Marawi

For decades the southern Philippines has been shaped by conflict, separatism, terrorism, and the search for peace. At the center of that history is Mindanao, where local grievances and identity-based movements eventually became connected to international extremist networks.

Mears traces the arc from the MNLF and MILF through the rise of the Abu Sayyaf Group, the return of fighters from Afghanistan, ties to Al-Qaeda and Jemaah Islamiyah, the ransom economy, and finally the ISIS-aligned coalition behind the 2017 Siege of Marawi.

The Author

Practitioner first. Historian second.

Dr. Paul Mears served as a U.S. Army Command Sergeant Major in Special Operations and Communications, commanded a USCG drug interdiction boat, rose from officer to Captain in Florida law enforcement, and later served as a Federal Air Marshal.

From 2014 to 2018 he trained police and special forces in Abu Dhabi and Dubai as a Professor of Global Security Studies. Today he works in executive protection and writes on the history of terrorism.
