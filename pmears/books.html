The Mindanao Shadow

From Separatism to the Black Flag

For decades the southern Philippines has been shaped by conflict, political struggle, separatism, terrorism, and the search for peace. At the center of that turbulent history is Mindanao, a region where local grievances and identity-based movements eventually became connected to international extremist networks.

Book Overview

Dr. Paul Mears traces the evolution of this conflict from the foundations of Moro resistance to the emergence of the Abu Sayyaf Group and its transformation over time.

The book begins with the historical and geographic conditions that shaped resistance in Mindanao, examining the development of the Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF) and Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF). It then follows the emergence of Abu Sayyaf and the ideological shift that moved part of the conflict beyond traditional separatist goals.

Mears explores how the return of fighters from Afghanistan, international financing networks, and connections with Al-Qaeda and Jemaah Islamiyah helped transform a regional insurgency into part of a broader transnational security problem.

The book also examines a later transformation: the movement from international jihadist ambitions toward kidnapping, ransom, and criminal financing. As Abu Sayyaf fragmented, a new chapter emerged with the rise of ISIS and its influence in Mindanao.

At the center of this transformation was the alliance between Isnilon Hapilon and the Maute Group, setting the stage for the 2017 Siege of Marawi. Mears examines the coalition behind the siege, the urban warfare that followed, the humanitarian consequences, and the international intelligence and technical support involved.

Finally, The Mindanao Shadow looks beyond the battlefield toward reconstruction and permanent peace: the MILF peace process and the Bangsamoro entity, governance, alienation, and the importance of inclusivity in addressing the conditions that allow conflict to persist.

From separatist movements to global jihadist networks, from the ransom economy to the rise of the black flag — a chronological examination of how Mindanao became a critical theater in the evolution of terrorism in Southeast Asia.
